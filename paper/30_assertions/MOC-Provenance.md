# MOC: Provenance

The structural side of the method, the grounding question. It covers what an anchor binds, how the chain from an output statement to its source location stays unbroken, and what this traceability does and does not guarantee. Two supporting strands situate the vault's own chain without being its subject, provenance as a modeled concept in W3C PROV and source binding as an established humanities practice in the critical apparatus.

- [[30_assertions/prov-models-entities-activities-agents]], the W3C core model of provenance as entities, activities, and agents with a layer of responsibility.
- [[30_assertions/prov-validity-is-internal-consistency]], validity in PROV means a coherent record and carries no judgment on content.
- [[30_assertions/prov-derivation-conditions-unspecified]], PROV records that an influence took place and leaves its determination outside the model.
- [[30_assertions/critical-apparatus-binds-reading-to-witnesses]], the editorial apparatus resolves each reading against a register of witnesses.

## Open questions

- How does an anchor behave when the source is revised rather than replaced?
- What makes a provenance record itself trustworthy when validity means internal consistency only?
- Who decides on what evidence that a derivation holds when the standard leaves this unspecified?
- What binds a reading to its witness when the witness is identified across documents or over time?
