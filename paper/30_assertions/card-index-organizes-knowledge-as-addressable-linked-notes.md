# The card index organizes knowledge as addressable linked notes

## Statement

Luhmann's card index organizes its records so that each card carries a number and with it a fixed position in the file that does not change over time, and a later card is inserted between two existing ones, the numbering allowing a nearly unlimited number of such insertions and producing card numbers of up to thirteen digits. On top of that fixed placement the collection carries a dense structure of references, in which a card names the number of another card that often lies thematically and spatially far away, estimated by a sample count at roughly twenty thousand such references in the first collection and thirty thousand in the second. The archival description names an internal system of linking among the four prerequisites for the collection's function of producing new knowledge, and it names the counter-case as well, since a note or a smaller subject area outside the web of references becomes lost irretrievably in the bulk of notes.
