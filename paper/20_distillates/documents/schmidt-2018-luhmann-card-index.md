# Distillate: Schmidt 2018, Luhmann's Card Index

An archival description of Niklas Luhmann's card index that states its addressing scheme, its reference structure and its keyword index as the mechanisms by which the collection could be queried at all.

## Core statements

- The card index consists of approximately 90,000 handwritten cards in A-6 format in two collections, the first of about 23,000 cards divided into 108 subject sections, the second of about 67,000 cards divided into eleven top-level sections with about 100 subsections. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b03]] ^s1
- Notes taken while reading were normally not put into the collection directly, nor filed in exactly the same way they had been taken; in the evening Luhmann transferred the often only rudimentary records of the day into new notes according to his special filing technique. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b04]] ^s2
- Four characteristics are named as the prerequisites for the collection's function of producing new knowledge: a specific system of organization, a method of card integration with specific rules of numbering, an internal system of linking, and a comprehensive keyword index. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b05]] ^s3
- Entries were organized on the principle that a card must have only some relation to the previous entry without an overarching system being kept in mind, so that the position of a subject within the collection reveals nothing about its theoretical importance. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b08]] ^s4
- Each card is assigned a number and thereby a fixed position in the file that does not change over time, and a card created later that pursues an aspect of card 1/1 is given the number 1/1a and inserted between 1/1 and 1/2. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b10]] ^s5
- The numbering system allows a nearly infinite number of cards to be inserted between two initially consecutive cards and produces card numbers of up to 13 digits. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b11]] ^s6
- Luhmann noted on one card the number of another card, often thematically and spatially far away, and a sample count suggests approximately 20,000 such references in the first collection and about 30,000 in the second. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b12]] ^s7
- Three types of linking are distinguished in the collections. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b13]] ^s8
- A collective reference is often found at the beginning of a section, can list up to 25 references and typically names the respective subject alongside the number, and points either to cards related by subject matter and in close proximity or to cards far apart in other sections of the collection, the latter being the normal case. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b15]] ^s9
- A single reference is made at a particular place within a normal note and in most cases points to a card located at an entirely different place in the file, frequently in the context of a completely different discussion. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b16]] ^s10
- Existing cards were regularly updated with additional references whenever the integration of new cards in other parts of the collection made it necessary. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b17]] ^s11
- A note or a smaller subject area that is not linked to the web of references becomes lost irretrievably in the bulk of notes, and parts of the file that are poorly linked tend to remain isolated. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b19]] ^s12
- The absence of a fixed system of order and of a table of contents turned the keyword index into the key tool for finding notes again and thereby for gaining access to the system of references. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b20]] ^s13
- The keyword index makes no claim to listing all cards that refer to a term; Luhmann typically listed one to four places, on the assumption that the other relevant entries could be identified via the internal system of references. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b21]] ^s14
- Accessing the file via the keyword index does not limit the search to that term, because the method of card integration and the system of linking open up a web of notes leading away from the original topic to subjects the user would not initially have associated with it. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b22]] ^s15
- The file contains not only validated knowledge but also reflects the thought process, including potential mistakes and blind alleys that were later revised, because the original cards always remained in the file and a new card with revisions could be added where needed. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b24]] ^s16

## Terms

- **Second memory**: the term by which the author of the cards described the collection, whose complexity and evolutionary history let it produce ideas that surprised him. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b05]]
- **Multiple storage**: the principle that the notes on a topic or concept may be filed in different places and different contexts, which provides different avenues of accessing it. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b09]]
- **Short cut / hub**: a reference that leads to a distant region of the file, and a card carrying a collection of references that functions as a node with an above-average number of links. [[10_markdown/documents/schmidt-2018-luhmann-card-index#^b18]]

## Open questions

- The source states that a reference is placed "at a particular place in a normal note", which implies that a card holds several distinguishable places, but it nowhere states how many thoughts a single card carries. Any claim that Luhmann's cards regularly bear more than one thought does not come from this source.
- The reference counts are given as an estimate from a sample count, and the source does not state the sampling procedure.
- The source describes a single archive and offers no comparison with other card indexes, so nothing in it decides which of its features are peculiar to this collection.

## Appraisal

Written by the scientific coordinator of the archival project that holds the estate, so the description rests on direct access to the material rather than on the published self-descriptions that earlier accounts had to rely on; the article names those self-descriptions as its supplementary sources. It is a shortened version of a book chapter and is explicit about the space limit, which shows in the numbering section, where the illustration is called an idealization and the exceptions are pushed into a footnote. For this vault the article is the source on the mechanics of fixed addressing and of a reference structure kept up to date as the collection grows, and the value of its counter-observation is comparable, that an unlinked note is unreachable in practice however well the addressing works.
