# Distillate: PROV-DM (W3C Recommendation 2013-04-30)

The W3C Recommendation that defines the PROV data model gives the vault a standardized vocabulary for provenance, its core types entity, activity and agent, and its explicit refusal to fix the conditions under which a derivation holds.

## Core statements

- Provenance is defined as a record that describes the people, institutions, entities, and activities involved in producing, influencing, or delivering a piece of data or a thing. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm01]] ^s1
- The provenance of information is treated as crucial for deciding whether information is to be trusted, how it should be integrated with other information sources, and how to give credit to its originators when reusing it. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm01]] ^s2
- PROV-DM is a generic data model that allows domain-specific and application-specific representations of provenance to be translated into it and interchanged between systems. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm02]] ^s3
- The PROV data model distinguishes core structures, which form the essence of provenance information, from extended structures, which refine them for more advanced uses. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm03]] ^s4
- The PROV data model is domain-agnostic and provides extensibility points for domain-specific and application-specific extensions. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm03]] ^s5
- The PROV data model is structured according to six components covering different facets of provenance. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm04]] ^s6
- PROV-DM presents its concepts, types and relations without specific concern for how they are applied, so that provenance can be written and published or embedded alongside the data it relates to. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm05]] ^s7
- Where the thing whose provenance is expressed is subject to change, expressing that provenance precisely is challenging, and the companion specification PROV-CONSTRAINTS addresses this by formal constraints and additional valid inferences. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm06]] ^s8
- The normative sections of PROV-DM are Section 1.1, Section 1.3, Section 5 and Appendix A. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm07]] ^s9
- At its core, provenance in PROV describes the use and production of entities by activities, which may be influenced in various ways by agents. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm09]] ^s10
- Names of PROV relations have a verbal form in the past tense to express what happened rather than what may or will happen, and in the core of PROV all relations are binary. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm10]] ^s11
- An entity is a physical, digital, conceptual, or other kind of thing with some fixed aspects, and entities may be real or imaginary. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm12]] ^s12
- An activity is something that occurs over a period of time and acts upon or with entities, and it may include consuming, processing, transforming, modifying, relocating, using, or generating them. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm13]] ^s13
- Generation is the completion of production of a new entity by an activity, and the entity did not exist before generation and becomes available for usage after it. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm15]] ^s14
- Usage is the beginning of utilizing an entity by an activity, and before usage the activity had not begun to utilize the entity and could not have been affected by it. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm16]] ^s15
- Communication is the exchange of some unspecified entity by two activities, one activity using some entity generated by the other. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm18]] ^s16
- A derivation is a transformation of an entity into another, an update of an entity resulting in a new one, or the construction of a new entity based on a pre-existing entity. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm20]] ^s17
- PROV does not attempt to specify the conditions under which derivations exist; derivation is considered to have been determined by unspecified means. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm21]] ^s18
- A chain of usage and generation is necessary but not sufficient for a derivation to hold between entities, because some form of influence occurring during the activities involved is also needed. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm21]] ^s19
- Knowing who or what was responsible for the production of something is treated as a key consideration for deciding whether it is reliable or trustworthy. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm22]] ^s20
- An agent is something that bears some form of responsibility for an activity taking place, for the existence of an entity, or for another agent's activity, and an agent may itself be a type of entity or activity, so that the provenance of agents can be expressed. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm23]] ^s21
- Attribution is the ascribing of an entity to an agent. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm25]] ^s22
- An activity association is an assignment of responsibility to an agent for an activity, indicating that the agent had a role in the activity. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm27]] ^s23
- Delegation is the assignment of authority and responsibility to an agent to carry out a specific activity as a delegate or representative, while the agent it acts on behalf of retains some responsibility for the outcome. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm28]] ^s24
- A derivation can be described at various levels of precision, from relating two entities alone up to making the derivation path through usage, activity and generation explicit, which aids analysis of provenance and facilitates provenance-based reproducibility. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm37]] ^s25
- With the PROV data model it is also possible to compose descriptions that would not make sense, such as an entity used before it was generated, and the constraints of the companion specification SHOULD be used by developers to compose valid provenance descriptions and by implementers of reasoning engines to check descriptions for problems. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm40]] ^s26
- PROV allows asserters to describe partial states of entities by means of attributes and associated values, whose values are expected to remain unchanged for some period of time. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm42]] ^s27

## Terms

- **Provenance**: a record that describes the people, institutions, entities, and activities involved in producing, influencing, or delivering a piece of data or a thing. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm01]]
- **Entity**: a physical, digital, conceptual, or other kind of thing with some fixed aspects; entities may be real or imaginary. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm12]]
- **Activity**: something that occurs over a period of time and acts upon or with entities. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm13]]
- **Agent**: something that bears some form of responsibility for an activity taking place, for the existence of an entity, or for another agent's activity. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm23]]
- **Generation**: the completion of production of a new entity by an activity. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm15]]
- **Usage**: the beginning of utilizing an entity by an activity. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm16]]
- **Derivation**: a transformation of an entity into another, an update of an entity resulting in a new one, or the construction of a new entity based on a pre-existing entity. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm20]]
- **Attribution**: the ascribing of an entity to an agent. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm25]]
- **Delegation**: the assignment of authority and responsibility to an agent to carry out a specific activity as a delegate or representative. [[10_markdown/documents/prov-dm-20130430#^dm28]]

## Open questions

- The specification states that derivation is determined by unspecified means without naming who determines it or against what evidence, so it leaves open how an asserter is to decide that a derivation holds.
- The specification treats provenance as an aid to trust judgements without saying what makes a provenance record itself trustworthy.
- The represented scope covers the core structures; the extended structures for bundles, alternate entities and collections are outside it, so statements about provenance of provenance are not covered here.

## Appraisal

A W3C Recommendation is a stable, consensus-based normative reference, and PROV-DM is the reference vocabulary this vault should use whenever it names entity, activity, agent, generation, usage and derivation, rather than inventing terms of its own. Its usefulness here lies less in the relation catalogue than in two of its own commitments. It defines provenance as a record about the actors and processes behind a thing, which is a bookkeeping notion and carries no claim that the record is true, and it explicitly refuses to fix the conditions under which a derivation holds. Both match the vault's separation of grounding from evidence, and the second is the point at which the anchor mechanics of this vault do something PROV deliberately leaves open, because an anchor names the concrete passage a statement was taken from.
