# Distillate: LoC Recommended Formats Statement 2025-2026

The Library of Congress states the criteria by which it ranks digital formats as preferred or acceptable, and applies that ranking to digital textual works and electronic serials, which is the ranking a plain-text-based archive has to place itself against.

## Core statements

- The RFS builds its format evaluation on two sets of criteria, global/community criteria taken from seven sustainability factors (Disclosure, Adoption, Transparency, Self-documentation including accessibility support, External dependencies, Impact of patents, Technical protection mechanisms) and local/institutional criteria estimating the resources the Library itself has for preserving the content. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-06]] ^s1
- A preferred format meets or exceeds the benchmarks for all relevant sustainability factors and can be managed and preserved with confidence in the Library's current systems. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-07]] ^s2
- An acceptable format meets only minimum acceptability across the benchmarks or fails some relevant sustainability factors, and the Library can manage it at a basic level, with greater capacity requiring further investment. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-08]] ^s3
- Since 2024 the RFS counts a format's potential to support digital accessibility as an evaluation criterion. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-02]] ^s4
- Among the accessibility features the RFS asks about is whether text content can be structured, as in XML, or tagged, as in PDF, for screen readers. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-03]] ^s5
- The RFS does not require a format's accessibility features to be enabled for the format to be included in Library collections. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-05]] ^s6
- The RFS provides guidance on identifying sets of formats and gives no instructions for receiving material into repositories or for the ongoing tasks of managing that content. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-10]] ^s7
- For digital textual works the preferred formats as received are XML-based markup formats with accessible DTD or schema, presentation stylesheets and explicitly stated character encoding, EPUB, of which EPUB3 is the most common version while the other versions are preferred formats as well, other widely used book DTDs or schemas such as TEI and DocBook, and the page-layout formats PDF/UA, PDF/A and highest-quality PDF. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-13]] ^s8
- For digital textual works plain text sits in the acceptable tier, inside the sixth and last group "Other formats" of an ordered preference list and after rich text format, therefore below XHTML or HTML, XML-based document formats, SGML, BITS and the page-layout formats. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-14]] ^s9
- For digital textual works the preferred character encodings are UTF-8, UTF-16 with BOM and US-ASCII on the first rank, followed by ISO 8859, while any other encoding is acceptable. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-12]] ^s10
- For electronic serials the preferred formats as received are content compliant with NISO JATS with presentation stylesheets and explicitly stated character encoding, and the page-layout formats PDF/UA, PDF/A and highest-quality PDF. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-21]] ^s11
- For electronic serials plain text likewise sits in the sixth and last group "Other formats" of the acceptable tier's ordered preference list. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-22]] ^s12
- For digital textual works the preferred metadata is that supported by the format, in standards-based forms such as ONIX for Books, XMP, MODS, METS or MARCXML, embedded in or accompanying the digital item, covering title, creator, publication or creation date, place of publication, publisher, ISBN or ISSN and contact information, and where available language, further identifiers, edition, subject descriptors and abstracts. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-17]] ^s13
- Files of digital textual works must contain no measures such as digital rights management technologies or encryption that control access to or prevent use of the digital work. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-19]] ^s14

## Terms

- **Preferred format**: a format meeting or exceeding the benchmarks for all relevant sustainability factors, which the Library can manage and preserve with confidence in current systems. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-07]]
- **Acceptable format**: a format meeting minimum acceptability across the benchmarks or failing some relevant sustainability factors, which the Library can manage at a basic level. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-08]]
- **Sustainability factors**: the seven global/community properties by which the RFS judges a format, Disclosure, Adoption, Transparency, Self-documentation, External dependencies, Impact of patents and Technical protection mechanisms. [[10_markdown/documents/loc-recommended-formats-2025-2026#^loc-06]]

## Open questions

- The RFS lists plain text within the acceptable tier without stating which sustainability factors plain text fails or only minimally meets, so the ranking cannot be traced back to the individual factor from this source.
- The local/institutional half of the criteria is stated for the Library of Congress alone, and the source does not say how another institution would substitute its own capacities.
- The statement is declared as geared towards published content, and it does not say how far its rankings carry to working material inside a research project.

## Appraisal

The RFS is the format policy of a national deposit library and carries the weight of an institution that must actually keep what it accepts, which makes its ranking a benchmark rather than an opinion. Its usefulness here lies in the fact that it ranks against a use case the Grounded Vault does not share. The Library ranks for deposit of published works, where structure, layout fidelity and rendering are part of what is deposited, and by that measure plain text lands near the bottom. A vault whose purpose is anchoring rather than deposit values other properties of the same format, so the ranking has to be read as evidence of a trade-off with a stated purpose behind it. The two-part criteria structure, global sustainability factors and local institutional capacity, is the part that transfers directly, because it names capacity of the holding institution as a criterion in its own right.
